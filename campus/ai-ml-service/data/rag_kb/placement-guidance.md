# Placement Guidance

## Writing resume bullets that prove a skill
A skill listed only in a Skills line is weak evidence. Recruiters and ATS filters trust skills that appear inside a bullet describing what you built. Use the pattern action verb + what you built + the technology + a measurable result, for example "Built a REST API in Flask with PostgreSQL that served 500 daily requests". Put the strongest, most role-relevant project first and keep every bullet to one or two lines.

## Making a resume pass ATS screening
Use standard section headings such as Education, Skills, Projects, Experience and Certifications. Use a single-column layout with plain text, avoid tables, text boxes and images for important content, and save as PDF unless the posting asks for another format. Mirror the exact skill names from the job description where they are true for you, for example write "PostgreSQL" if the posting says PostgreSQL rather than only "databases".

## Turning a skill gap into a plan
Close one gap at a time. Pick the missing skill that appears most often in postings for your target role, learn the fundamentals in one or two weeks, then build a small project that uses it and add a bullet to your resume. A short project with a visible result is stronger evidence than a certificate alone. Revisit the gap list after each project.

## Structuring interview answers
Behavioural questions are answered best with the STAR structure: the Situation, the Task you owned, the Action you personally took, and the Result. Prepare four or five stories from projects, internships or teamwork that can be reused for questions on conflict, failure, leadership, deadlines and learning something new. For technical questions, state your approach before coding, discuss complexity, and test with a small example.

## Data structures and algorithms preparation
Build a steady routine rather than cramming: arrays and strings first, then hashing, two pointers and sliding window, recursion and backtracking, trees and graphs, and finally dynamic programming. Solve problems by topic, write down the pattern behind each solution, and revisit missed problems after a week. Practise explaining your solution aloud because interviews test communication as much as correctness.

## Aptitude and communication
Many campus drives start with an aptitude round covering quantitative ability, logical reasoning and verbal ability. Short daily timed practice beats long irregular sessions. For communication, rehearse a 60-second introduction covering your education, strongest project and the role you want, and practise it until it sounds natural rather than memorised.

## Projects that strengthen a profile
Choose projects that resemble the work of your target role, deploy at least one so a reviewer can open a live link, and keep the code on a public repository with a clear README. Describe the problem, your design decisions and the result, and be ready to explain every part of the project in an interview.

## Moving between private and government career tracks
Skills such as aptitude, reasoning, computer fundamentals, databases and networking carry across private-sector placements and government technical recruitment. Official vacancies, eligibility, age limits and dates are published by the recruiting bodies and shown in the portal's verified Govt Jobs records, so check those records rather than relying on any general guidance.
